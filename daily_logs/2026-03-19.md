**Topics:** Dsilva et al. 2022/2023 paper analysis, bias correction methodology comparison, single star simulation, likelihood degeneracy at f_bin=1
**Work done:**
- Read Dsilva+23 (Paper III, A&A 674, A88) and Dsilva+22 (Paper II, A&A 664, A93) in full
- Identified how Dsilva handles single stars: they are NOT simulated — singles have ΔRV=0, wind variability absorbed by threshold C=50 km/s (3× observed ~15 km/s)
- Compared Dsilva methodology to our implementation in `wr_bias_simulation.py`
- Reviewed our `multinomial_log_likelihood()` function and confirmed it matches Dsilva's binned approach
- Discussed f_bin=1 degeneracy in likelihood: the coarse bins [0, 45.5, 250, 650, ∞] cannot distinguish true singles from undetected long-period binaries, creating a ridge in (f_bin, π) space
- Identified that Dsilva breaks this degeneracy with prior information on known orbital periods (enforcing minimum counts in period bins)
**Decisions:**
- Our σ_single parameter being insensitive to likelihood is actually consistent with Dsilva (they don't model it at all)
- The f_bin=1 degeneracy is a methodological issue with coarse binning, not a bug — need to investigate whether period-bin priors or K-S test (continuous CDF) break it

- Do we implement Dsilva-style period-bin priors (enforcing known binaries have P>1d, P>10d etc.)?
- Should we use finer bins within the 0–45.5 range to capture the shape difference between singles and undetected binaries?
