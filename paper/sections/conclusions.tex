\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have presented a multi-epoch VLT/X-Shooter spectroscopic multiplicity
survey of \Nstars apparently single carbon-rich Wolf--Rayet stars in the
Large Magellanic Cloud, combined with the \Nknown previously known
WC/WO binaries from \citet{Bartzakos2001}, for a total sample of
\Ntotal stars. Our main findings are:

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Sample and observed binary fraction.} Multi-epoch
  X-Shooter spectroscopy of \Nstars apparently single LMC WC/WO stars
  (plus \Nknown previously known binaries from
  \citealt{Bartzakos2001}) yields an observed binary fraction of
  \fbinobs ($\Nbin/\Ntotal$). The detection criterion is anchored on
  the \primaryline emission line, the strongest and least variable WC
  wind feature; an independent line-agreement cross-check
  (Sect.~\ref{sec:line_agreement}) confirms its primacy by showing
  that per-line RV self-consistency across the sample correlates
  positively with emission-line strength.

\item \textbf{Bias-corrected intrinsic fraction (Dsilva power-law
  model).} Following the cadence-aware Monte-Carlo bias-correction
  methodology of \citet{Dsilva2023} and using a multinomial-likelihood
  comparison on $\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max}$ bins
  (Eq.~\ref{eq:multinomial}), we will constrain the intrinsic binary
  fraction to $f_\mathrm{bin} = \fbincorr^{+\fbinerrup}_{-\fbinerrdn}$
  and the orbital-period-distribution exponent to
  $\pi = \pibestfit^{+\pierrup}_{-\pierrdn}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:bias}).
  Reported uncertainties are 68\% highest-density intervals from the
  marginalised log-likelihood.

\item \textbf{Cross-checks.} A second period model based on the
  two-component binary-evolution channels of \citet{Langer2020}
  (Sect.~\ref{sec:langer_model}), a non-parametric threshold-curve fit
  using a simulation-derived binary $\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max}$
  distribution (Sect.~\ref{sec:threshold_fit}), and a bin-choice
  sensitivity envelope across multiple $\Delta\mathrm{RV}$-binning
  schemes will all be tested for consistency with the headline values
  within the 68\% HDI. To our knowledge, the bin-sensitivity analysis
  is the first quantitative validation of binning robustness in a WR
  multiplicity survey.

\item \textbf{Methodological caveats.} The small sample size
  ($N = \Ntotal$) places the multinomial likelihood in the
  Poisson-noise-limited regime, so reported uncertainties are 68\% HDIs
  from the joint posterior rather than Gaussian standard errors. The
  $\sim$3-semester observing baseline limits sensitivity to orbital
  periods longer than $\sim 10$\,yr, leaving wide-orbit binaries as a
  known incompleteness gap. The single-star RV scatter is poorly
  constrained by the binned likelihood and is held fixed at a
  representative value; the parametric Gaussian threshold derivation
  of Sect.~\ref{sec:threshold_derivation} captures the bulk
  $\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max}$ distribution but is approximate in the
  wings, motivating the simulation-derived refinement of
  Sect.~\ref{sec:threshold_fit}.

\item \textbf{Implications for WC/WO formation channels.} The
  bias-corrected fraction \fbincorr provides a direct empirical test of
  the metallicity-dependent binary-channel hypothesis: a value above
  the corresponding Galactic estimate of \citet{Dsilva2023} would
  support the prediction that line-driven mass loss is too weak at LMC
  metallicity for the single-star channel to dominate
  \citep{Shenar2020,Marchant2023}, while a value comparable to or below
  the Galactic value would constrain it. A definitive verdict awaits
  finalisation of the bias-correction grid (Sect.~\ref{sec:bias}).

\end{enumerate}

Three planned extensions of the present analysis will be reported in
companion papers: (i) a refined threshold-curve fit using a
non-Gaussian, simulation-derived parametric form for the binary
$\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max}$ distribution
(Sect.~\ref{sec:threshold_fit}), enabled by the converged
bias-correction parameters; (ii) photospheric-absorption confirmation
of all \Ndetected newly classified binaries together with a search for
hidden binaries among the apparently single stars
(Sect.~\ref{sec:spec_morphology}); and (iii) phase-resolved
spectroscopic monitoring of the \NcandidateBH candidate WC + compact-object
systems (Sect.~\ref{sec:compact_objects}) to constrain the black-hole
mass distribution from stripped-helium-star progenitors.
