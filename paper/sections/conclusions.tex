\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have presented a multi-epoch VLT/X-Shooter spectroscopic multiplicity
survey of \Nstars apparently single carbon-rich Wolf--Rayet stars in the
Large Magellanic Cloud, combined with the \Nknown previously known
WC/WO binaries from \citet{Bartzakos2001}, for a total sample of
\Ntotal stars.

The radial-velocity analysis is anchored on the \primaryline emission
line --- the strongest and least variable WC wind feature, whose primacy
is confirmed independently by a line-agreement cross-check
(Sect.~\ref{sec:line_agreement}) showing that per-line RV
self-consistency correlates positively with emission-line strength.
Applying the joint $\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max} > \drvthresh$\,\kms and
$\sigthresh\sigma$ criterion of Sect.~\ref{sec:criteria}, we detect
\Ndetected new spectroscopic binaries, raising the total to \Nbin out of
\Ntotal and yielding an observed binary fraction of \fbinobs.
Following the cadence-aware Monte-Carlo bias-correction methodology of
\citet{Dsilva2023} and a multinomial-likelihood comparison on
$\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max}$ bins (Eq.~\ref{eq:multinomial}), the
intrinsic binary fraction is constrained to
$f_\mathrm{bin} = {\fbincorr}^{+\fbinerrup}_{-\fbinerrdn}$ and the
orbital-period-distribution exponent to
$\pi = {\pibestfit}^{+\pierrup}_{-\pierrdn}$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:bias}),
with 68\% highest-density intervals derived from the marginalised
log-likelihood.

Three orthogonal cross-checks bracket this result. The
two-component binary-evolution period model of \citet{Langer2020}
(Sect.~\ref{sec:langer_model}), the non-parametric threshold-curve fit
using a simulation-derived binary $\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max}$
distribution (Sect.~\ref{sec:threshold_fit}), and the bin-choice
sensitivity envelope across multiple $\Delta\mathrm{RV}$-binning
schemes are all tested for consistency with the headline values within
the 68\% HDI. To our knowledge, the bin-sensitivity analysis is the
first quantitative validation of binning robustness in a WR
multiplicity survey.

Several methodological caveats temper the headline values. The small
sample size ($N = \Ntotal$) places the multinomial likelihood in the
Poisson-noise-limited regime, so reported uncertainties are 68\% HDIs
from the joint posterior rather than Gaussian standard errors, and the
$\sim$3-semester observing baseline limits sensitivity to orbital
periods longer than $\sim 10$\,yr, leaving wide-orbit binaries as a
known incompleteness gap. The single-star RV scatter is poorly
constrained by the binned likelihood and is held fixed at a
representative value; the parametric Gaussian threshold derivation of
Sect.~\ref{sec:threshold_derivation} captures the bulk
$\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max}$ distribution but is approximate in the
wings, motivating the simulation-derived refinement of
Sect.~\ref{sec:threshold_fit}.

The bias-corrected fraction \fbincorr provides a direct empirical test
of the metallicity-dependent binary-channel hypothesis. A value above
the corresponding Galactic estimate of \citet{Dsilva2023} would support
the prediction that line-driven mass loss is too weak at LMC
metallicity for the single-star channel to dominate
\citep{Shenar2020, Marchant2023}, while a value comparable to or below
the Galactic value would constrain it. A definitive verdict awaits
finalisation of the bias-correction grid (Sect.~\ref{sec:bias}).

Three planned extensions of the present analysis will be reported in
companion papers: (i) a refined threshold-curve fit using a
non-Gaussian, simulation-derived parametric form for the binary
$\Delta\mathrm{RV}_{\max}$ distribution
(Sect.~\ref{sec:threshold_fit}), enabled by the converged
bias-correction parameters; (ii) photospheric-absorption confirmation
of all \Ndetected newly classified binaries together with a search for
hidden binaries among the apparently single stars
(Sect.~\ref{sec:spec_morphology}); and (iii) phase-resolved
spectroscopic monitoring of the \NcandidateBH candidate WC + compact-object
systems (Sect.~\ref{sec:compact_objects}) to constrain the black-hole
mass distribution from stripped-helium-star progenitors.
